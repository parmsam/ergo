# Walking and activity breaks from sitting

**Used in ergo:** body rules "5 min walk / 30", "2 min walk / 20" and "3 min active / 30" (half-squats, calf raises, knee raises, glute squeezes or a walk); coaching after long unbroken sitting; the weekly activity checklist item.
**Strength:** Trial evidence (`moderate`). Randomized crossover trials show short-term metabolic benefits. Samples are small and the outcomes are acute.

## What the research says

- **2 min every 20 min (Dunstan 2012).** In a randomized crossover trial, 19 overweight/obese adults aged 45–65 sat uninterrupted, or broke up sitting with 2 min walking bouts (light or moderate) every 20 min [1]. Both walking conditions **lowered post-meal glucose and insulin** compared with uninterrupted sitting.
- **5 min every 30 min (Duran 2023).** 11 middle- and older-aged adults completed 8-hour days of uninterrupted sitting, plus four break schedules: 1 or 5 min of light walking, every 30 or 60 min [2]. **Only 5 min every 30 min significantly reduced the glucose response**, cutting post-meal spikes by about 58% versus sitting all day. All break schedules lowered systolic blood pressure.
- **3 min every 30 min, walking *or* simple strength moves (Dempsey 2016).**
  - Randomized crossover trial in 24 inactive, overweight or obese adults with type 2 diabetes.
  - Each person did three 8-hour days: uninterrupted sitting; 3 min of light walking every 30 min; or 3 min of simple resistance activities every 30 min. The resistance moves were half-squats, calf raises, gluteal contractions and knee raises, with no equipment [4].
  - **Both** activity conditions reduced post-meal glucose, insulin and C-peptide responses versus sitting.
  - **Only the resistance activities** also significantly reduced triglycerides.
  - A companion analysis reported lower resting blood pressure with both, with resistance activities more effective [5].
- **Guidelines.** The WHO 2020 guidelines recommend that adults limit sedentary time and replace it with physical activity of any intensity, alongside 150–300 min/week of moderate activity. The evidence didn't support a specific sedentary-time threshold [3].

## How ergo applies it

- Walk breaks use light, easy walking, the intensity used in the studies.
- The "5 min walk / 30" rule matches the schedule that performed best in Duran 2023.
- The "3 min active / 30" rule follows Dempsey 2016. Its break screen rotates through the same no-equipment moves, so you don't have to leave your desk.
- The coach calls it out when you've been running ergo for two hours without standing or moving.

## Caveats

- Dempsey 2016 studied adults with type 2 diabetes; effects in healthy adults may be smaller.
- All three trials were small, acute (single-day) lab studies measuring metabolic markers. They don't show long-term disease outcomes.
- Breaks complement exercise; they don't replace it.
