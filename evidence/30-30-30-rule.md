# The 30-30-30 rule

**Used in ergo:** optional eye rule. Every 30 minutes, look at something about 30 feet (9 m) away for 30 seconds.
**Strength:** Expert guidance (`expert`). Recommended by clinicians as a variant of 20-20-20. No trials of this specific schedule were found.

## What the research says

- It is promoted by ophthalmologists as a simple habit against digital eye strain: every 30 minutes, look at an object 30 feet away for 30 seconds [1]. The source refers to research only in general terms and cites no specific studies.
- It trades frequency for duration: fewer interruptions than 20-20-20, with a longer look-away each time. Critics of 20-20-20 have suggested that longer breaks may matter more than the exact interval (see [20-20-20-rule.md](20-20-20-rule.md)).
- The general idea is supported by the same evidence as other break schedules: reminders to take breaks improved symptoms while they were in use (Talens-Estarelles 2023; see [20-20-20-rule.md](20-20-20-rule.md)).

## How ergo applies it

Offered for people who find 20-minute interruptions too frequent. Pair it with the blink nudge if your eyes feel dry.

## Caveats

No trial has compared 30-30-30 with other schedules. Treat it as a reasonable, gentler cadence rather than a proven one.
