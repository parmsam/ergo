# 20-8-2: sit, stand, move

**Used in ergo:** default body rule. In each half hour: sit 20 min, stand 8 min (a standing cue, keep working), then move 2 min (a break with stretches or a short walk). Also backs the "way to stand while working" checklist item.
**Strength:** Expert guidance (`expert`). A research-informed rhythm from a leading ergonomist; the exact split hasn't been tested head-to-head.

## What the research says

- **The rule.** Cornell ergonomist Prof. Alan Hedge recommends 20 minutes sitting in a good posture, 8 minutes standing (for sit-stand workstations) and 2 minutes standing and moving (gentle stretching, walking), **as a ballpark goal** [1]. Over a 7.5-hour workday that comes to about 5 h sitting, 16 sit-to-stand changes, 2 h standing and 30 min moving.
- **Neither extreme is good.** Hedge's point is that both sitting all day and standing all day are bad for you. Standing for more than about 10 minutes at a time tends to lead to leaning and slouching, so standing bouts are kept short [1].
- **Expert consensus on office sitting.** A 2015 expert statement commissioned by Public Health England (Buckley et al., co-authored by Hedge) recommends working toward **2 hours a day** of standing and light activity during working hours, **progressing to 4 hours**. It also recommends regularly breaking up seated work with standing work, and avoiding prolonged static standing [2].

## How ergo applies it

- The standing phase is a **cue**, not a break: ergo prompts you to stand and keep working, and you can tap "I'm standing" to log it.
- The 2-minute move is a **break** with guided stretches or a walk.
- No standing desk? Use a custom body rule with stand = 0 (sit / move only), or take calls standing.

## Caveats

- The 20/8/2 numbers are a practical recommendation, not the result of a trial comparing splits.
- Standing isn't exercise. Movement (walking, changing position) is what the evidence most supports; see [walking-breaks.md](walking-breaks.md).
