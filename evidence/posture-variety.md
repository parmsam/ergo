# Posture: variety over perfection

**Used in ergo:** the posture-reset nudge (default every 15 min), the "back supported" checklist item, and how ergo talks about posture generally.
**Strength:** Expert guidance (`expert`).

## What the research says

- **There's no single "correct" posture.** Physiotherapy researchers have challenged the idea that sitting bolt upright prevents back pain. They argue there's little evidence that any one posture is protective, and that comfort, variety and movement matter more than holding a "perfect" position [1].
- **Static postures are the problem.** Ergonomics guidance consistently emphasizes changing position. Hedge's 20-8-2 rhythm builds in about 16 posture changes a day [2], and the 2015 expert statement recommends breaking up seated work and avoiding prolonged static standing [3].
- **Support still helps.** OSHA's workstation guidance recommends a chair that supports the lower back, feet flat or on a footrest, and relaxed shoulders with elbows close to the body [4].

## How ergo applies it

- The posture nudge says "shift position", not "sit up straight": drop the shoulders, ease the head back, plant the feet, or simply change how you're sitting.
- Body breaks and standing cues add bigger changes through the day.

## Caveats

- The nudge interval (15 min) is a sensible default rather than a researched number. Adjust it, or turn it off if you already move often.
