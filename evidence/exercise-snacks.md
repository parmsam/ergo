# Exercise snacks (short bursts of vigorous activity)

**Used in ergo:** the optional "Exercise snack" nudge (off by default; every 120 min when on) prompting one vigorous minute: brisk stairs, a fast uphill walk, carrying something heavy. "Did it" logs it.
**Strength:** Observational (`observational`). A large wearable-device cohort study shows a strong association with lower mortality, but it can't prove cause and effect.

## What the research says

- **VILPA study (Stamatakis et al. 2022, *Nature Medicine*)** [1]:
  - Studied 25,241 adults (mean age 61.8) who reported **no leisure-time exercise**, using wrist accelerometers to capture **vigorous intermittent lifestyle physical activity**: short bursts of vigorous effort during daily life, such as fast stair climbing or brisk walking.
  - About 6.9 years of follow-up (852 deaths).
  - At the median of **3 bouts a day, each 1–2 minutes**, risk was **38–40% lower for all-cause and cancer mortality** and **48–49% lower for cardiovascular mortality**, compared with no such bursts.
  - The association was near-linear: more bouts, lower risk.
- The authors suggest these "exercise snacks" may be a practical option for people who don't or can't do structured exercise.

## How ergo applies it

- Off by default. When on, a cue every 2 hours (about 3–4 a workday, close to the study's median) suggests one vigorous minute.
- Logged separately from breaks, and doesn't affect break adherence or the ergo score.

## Caveats

- **Observational.** People who move vigorously in daily life may be healthier in other ways. The study adjusted for many factors, but this isn't a trial.
- "Vigorous" means you're breathing hard: harder than the light walking in break trials.
- **If you have a heart condition, high blood pressure, or haven't been active, check with a clinician first.** The settings note says so too.
