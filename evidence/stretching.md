# Stretching and desk exercises

**Used in ergo:** the exercise library and the guided steps on break screens.
**Strength:** Mixed evidence (`limited`) for specific stretches. Breaks themselves are better supported than what you do in them.

## What the research says

- In the NIOSH follow-up study, adding stretching exercises to supplementary breaks showed **no significant additional effect** on discomfort. Low compliance (few people did the stretches) made it hard to judge [1].
- The breaks themselves did reduce discomfort and eyestrain [1, 2]. Getting up and changing position is the part with consistent support.
- Light walking has measurable short-term metabolic benefits (see [walking-breaks.md](walking-breaks.md)).

## How ergo applies it

- Exercises are **gentle, low-risk and optional**. They give you something easy to do with a break, so you're more likely to take it.
- Walking and standing exercises are favored for body breaks; far-gaze, blinking and palming for eye breaks.
- Instructions say to stretch gently and never into pain.

## Caveats

- If you have an injury or a condition affecting your joints, nerves or eyes, ask a clinician which movements suit you.
