# Blinking and dry eye

**Used in ergo:** the optional blink nudge (default every 10 min), blink drills during eye breaks, and the dry-eye checklist item.
**Strength:** Trial evidence (`moderate`) that screen use cuts blinking. Expert guidance for the remedies.

## What the research says

- **Screens suppress blinking.** Patel et al. (1991) measured a roughly fivefold drop in blink rate during visual display unit use in healthy subjects [1].
- The AAO puts it this way: people normally blink about **15 times a minute**, but only about **5 to 7 times a minute** while using screens. That dries the eyes [2].
- **AAO remedies** for screen-related eye strain [2]:
  - Use artificial tears when your eyes feel dry.
  - Use a humidifier in dry, warm rooms.
  - Position the screen so your gaze is slightly downward.
  - Take breaks to look far away.

## How ergo applies it

- An optional blink nudge reminds you to blink slowly and fully.
- Eye breaks include "slow full blinks" in the exercise rotation.
- The workstation checklist covers artificial tears, humidity and screen height.

## Caveats

- Evidence that blink *reminders* reduce symptoms long-term is limited. The nudge is cheap and low-risk, so it's offered as an option (off by default).
- Persistent dry eye deserves an eye exam; drops aren't a substitute for diagnosis.
