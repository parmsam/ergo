# Blinking and dry eye

**Used in ergo:** "slow full blinks" during eye breaks, the dry-eye checklist item, and a fact in the Guide. (A periodic blink nudge existed in v0.1.0 and was removed in v0.2.0; see below.)
**Strength:** Trial evidence (`moderate`) that screen use cuts blinking. Expert guidance for the remedies.

## What the research says

- **Screens suppress blinking.** Patel et al. (1991) measured a roughly fivefold drop in blink rate during visual display unit use in healthy subjects [1].
- The AAO puts it this way: people normally blink about **15 times a minute**, but only about **5 to 7 times a minute** while using screens. That dries the eyes [2].
- **Blink prompts can help.** In a small crossover trial (24 computer users, one week per arm), on-screen "blink blink" animations asked people to blink twice whenever they appeared. Compared with a placebo version of the software, the animations raised blink rate by about 6.8 blinks/min and improved dry-eye symptoms more [3]. Symptoms improved somewhat with the placebo too, and 20 of 24 people tolerated the prompts well.
- **AAO remedies** for screen-related eye strain [2]:
  - Use artificial tears when your eyes feel dry.
  - Use a humidifier in dry, warm rooms.
  - Position the screen so your gaze is slightly downward.
  - Take breaks to look far away.

## How ergo applies it

- Eye breaks include "slow full blinks" in the exercise rotation.
- **No periodic blink nudge.** People normally blink 15–20 times a minute; the problem during screen work is fewer and incomplete blinks over the whole session. A reminder every few minutes doesn't match that, and the trial above used frequent on-screen animations, not periodic reminders. So ergo leaves blinking to eye breaks and the dry-eye tips.
- Eye breaks include "slow full blinks" in the exercise rotation.
- The workstation checklist covers artificial tears, humidity and screen height.

## Caveats

- The prompt trial was small and lasted one week; long-term benefit is unknown.
- Persistent dry eye deserves an eye exam; drops aren't a substitute for diagnosis.
