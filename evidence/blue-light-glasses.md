# Blue-light filtering glasses (myth)

**Used in ergo:** the Guide's Myths section. ergo doesn't recommend blue-light glasses for eye strain.
**Strength:** Systematic review: a Cochrane review of randomized trials, though the trials were small and certainty is limited.

## What the research says

- **Cochrane review (Singh et al. 2023)** [1]:
  - Pooled **17 randomized trials** (619 participants, six countries).
  - Found that blue-light filtering lenses **may not reduce short-term eye strain** from computer use, compared with ordinary lenses.
  - Effects on vision quality and sleep were unclear.
  - No conclusions could be drawn about long-term retinal health, because the trials didn't measure it.
  - Side effects were mild and temporary.
- Digital eye strain comes mainly from **focusing effort** and **reduced blinking** (see [digital-eye-strain.md](digital-eye-strain.md) and [blinking-dry-eye.md](blinking-dry-eye.md)), not from blue light.

## How ergo applies it

The Guide points people to breaks, blinking, an up-to-date prescription and screen setup instead.

## Caveats

- The included trials were small and short, and the authors called for larger, longer studies.
- This is about eye strain. Questions about screens before bed and sleep are a separate topic.
