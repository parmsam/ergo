# The 20-20-20 rule

**Used in ergo:** default eye rule. Every 20 minutes of screen time, a 20-second break to look at something about 20 feet (6 m) away. Also the basis of the custom eye rule.
**Strength:** Mixed evidence (`limited`). Break reminders helped in a trial, but the specific numbers have been questioned and the benefit faded once reminders stopped.

## What the research says

- **It's widely recommended.** Eye-care professionals and the American Academy of Ophthalmology (AAO) regularly advise looking away at a distance during screen work. The AAO's patient page says to look at a distant object "for a bit" and to keep the screen about 25 in away [1].
- **Reminders reduced symptoms in a 2023 trial.** Talens-Estarelles et al. gave 29 symptomatic computer users reminder software for two weeks [2]. Breaks became more frequent and continuous screen bouts shorter. Digital eye strain and dry-eye symptom scores improved during the reminder period, but **the improvement didn't persist one week after the reminders stopped**. Most binocular-vision measures didn't change (accommodative facility improved).
- **The numbers themselves are debated.** Johnson & Rosenfield (2023) tested several break schedules during a demanding task. They found little support for the 20-20-20 specifics and noted how little peer-reviewed evidence backs them [3]. Others have argued 20 seconds may be too short to fully relax focusing and convergence.

## How ergo applies it

- Look-away breaks are short and frequent, so they're easy to keep.
- Because benefits faded when reminders stopped [2], ergo is built around **sustained** reminders and tracks follow-through over time.
- The break screen nudges you to also blink fully (see [blinking-dry-eye.md](blinking-dry-eye.md)).
- If 20 s feels too short, use the 30-30-30 rule or a custom rule with a longer break, or add the hourly microbreak (see [microbreaks.md](microbreaks.md)).

## Caveats

- The trial was small (29 people) and short (2 weeks).
- "20 feet" is a convenient distance, not a magic one. Anything across the room or out a window works.
